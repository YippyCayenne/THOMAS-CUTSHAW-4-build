AIP Chicken Meal

Introduction

This is a simple recipe for an autoimmune protocol (AIP) friendly chicken dish. The wholesome baked chicken, sweet potato, and asparagus meal combines tender, seasoned chicken with naturally sweet roasted potatoes and crisp, flavorful asparagus. Everything cooks together in the oven for an easy, satisfying dinner that delivers a delicious balance of savory, sweet, and fresh flavors. It’s a simple, nourishing meal that’s perfect for busy weeknights or meal prep.

The Sweet Potato

Begin your meal prep with the sweet potato because it takes the longest to cook. Here's how to prepare it:

  1. Preheat: Heat your oven to 400°F (200°C).
  2. Prep: Wash and scrub the sweet potatoes, then pat them dry. Pierce each one several times with a fork.
  3. Season: Rub the potatoes lightly with olive oil and sprinkle with a little salt.
  4. Bake: Place them on a baking sheet and bake for 45–60 minutes, or until tender when pierced with a fork.
  5. Serve: Let them cool for a few minutes, then slice open and add your favorite toppings, such as butter, cinnamon, or a drizzle of honey.

The Chicken

When the sweet potatoes have about 35 minutes left in the oven, begin preparing the chicken. Here's how to do it:

  1. Preheat: Heat your oven to 400°F (200°C).
  2. Season: Pat chicken breasts dry and season both sides with olive oil, salt, pepper, garlic powder, and your favorite herbs.
  3. Arrange: Place the chicken in a lightly greased baking dish, leaving space between each piece.
  4. Bake: Bake for about 20–25 minutes, or until the thickest part reaches 165°F (74°C).
  5. Rest & serve: Let the chicken rest for 5 minutes before slicing and serving.

The Asparagus

As soon as the chicken is in the oven, prepare the asparagus:

  1. Preheat: Heat your oven to 400°F (200°C).
  2. Prep: Rinse the asparagus and trim off the tough ends.
  3. Season: Place the asparagus on a baking sheet and toss with olive oil, salt, pepper, and your favorite herbs.
  4. Roast: Bake for 10–15 minutes, until the asparagus is tender and lightly browned.
  5. Serve: Remove from the oven and serve warm with a squeeze of fresh lemon juice, if desired.

What is AIP?

The Autoimmune Protocol (AIP) diet is an elimination-style eating approach designed to identify foods that may contribute to symptoms in people with certain autoimmune conditions. It typically emphasizes nutrient-dense foods such as vegetables, fruits, quality proteins, and healthy fats while temporarily avoiding foods such as grains, legumes, dairy, eggs, nuts, seeds, nightshade vegetables, alcohol, and highly processed foods. After the elimination phase, foods are gradually reintroduced to help identify individual triggers. Because dietary needs and responses vary, AIP is best approached with guidance from a qualified healthcare professional or registered dietitian.
